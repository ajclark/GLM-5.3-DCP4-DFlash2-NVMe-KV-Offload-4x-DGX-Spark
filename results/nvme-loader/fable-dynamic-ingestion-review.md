# Fable review transcript

Independent review, including unvalidated proposals and timing estimates. See the investigation document for accepted findings and corrections.

**Bottom line:** dynamic ingestion of the original shards can replace the 358 s native load with roughly a 60 to 110 s first-use path, but it cannot approach the 8 s prepared restore on a single node, because the amplification floor is set by which dimension each tensor is sharded on, not by the reader. Keep artifacts as the routine path; build the dynamic path as the first-use and generic fallback path behind the existing `DefaultModelLoader` subclass. Details below, with the drop-in runai question answered in finding 5.

## Findings

**1. Source amplification has a hard floor from the sharding dimension, and your header scan pins it at about 2x for GLM.**
The installed MoE loader at `results/nvme-loader/inventory/model_executor/layers/fused_moe/routed_experts.py:663` transposes compressed-tensors expert weights first, then flips the shard dim at line 676 and narrows by `tp_rank` at lines 510 and 552. In checkpoint coordinates that means gate and up slices are contiguous row blocks of the original tensor, while the down slice is 64 of 256 int32 columns in every row, exactly the 256 B of each 1 KiB row you computed. Dense o_proj and the dense-layer down_proj shard the same way. From `results/nvme-loader/checkpoint-layout.json`, experts are 383.4 GB of 405.2 GB, two thirds of that in gate and up:

| Path | Bytes read per rank | At observed 12.6 GB/s | At planning 4 to 5 GB/s |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Native or plain streamer, all bytes | 405 GB | 32 s | 81 to 101 s |
| Local byte-range plan, slice where contiguous | about 200 GB | 16 s | 40 to 50 s |
| Cross-node owner reads plus sliced scatter | 101 GB disk, about 76 GB inbound network | 8 s disk plus network | 20 to 25 s disk plus network |
| Prepared artifact today | 101 GB | 8 s measured | |

Per-row slices below 4 KiB must be read as whole rows, so "adaptive" means: slice when the plan yields a contiguous extent, read the whole tensor otherwise, and coalesce the tiny `weight_shape` and `weight_scale` tensors that safetensors packs adjacently.

**2. I/O is no longer the dominant cost on the dynamic path; the single-threaded consumer is.**
The installed model loop at `results/nvme-loader/inventory/models/deepseek_v32/nvidia/model.py:375` scans `expert_params_mapping` linearly for every expert tensor. With 256 experts times three projections that is about 768 entries, and the layout scan counts 175,104 expert-named tensors, so the substring scan alone is on the order of 60 to 70 million Python iterations, roughly 15 to 25 s per rank. On top of that the loader transposes about 380 GB of packed int32 through `.t().contiguous()` at line 663 and narrows and copies each result. The 3-D stacked load path at line 678 would collapse the tensor count, but the transpose at line 663 runs before that check and `t()` rejects 3-D tensors, so stacking is not available for this quantization without a loader change. Two consequences: the reader should yield device tensors so the transposes and copies run at memory bandwidth instead of on the CPU, and the Python floor of roughly 20 s is a permanent tax of the dynamic path that the artifact path never pays.

**3. Native multithread mmap and prefetch are not I/O concurrency you can use here.**
`multi_thread_safetensors_weights_iterator` at `results/nvme-loader/inventory/model_executor/model_loader/weight_utils.py:971` calls `load_file` per shard on a thread pool, which materializes whole shards in pageable CPU memory; the largest shard is 4.99 GB, eight threads can hold 30 to 40 GB, on a node reporting about 14 GiB available at load time. It also drops the EP filter and the load-strategy options. `_prefetch_all_checkpoints` at line 759 reads whole files sequentially into the page cache, splits files by rank at line 776 on the assumption of shared storage, and has no bounded window, so on rank-local disks it prefetches a quarter of the files into a cache that cannot hold them. Neither is chunked, positional, bounded I/O.

**4. Buffer lifetime must be refcount-owned, not slot-recycled, on the dynamic path.**
Unlike the artifact transport, the native consumer may retain what it is given: the pending FP8 indexer buffer at `model.py:337`, transposed temporaries, and in other models shared-expert fusion buffers. The installed runai iterator handles this by cloning every tensor at `weight_utils.py:1035`, which your fixture confirmed but which costs a full extra memcpy. The right generic rule is to yield owning tensors from a caching allocator, keep a byte semaphore of tensors yielded and not yet freed, and release through a weakref finalizer. For this model the loader retains nothing beyond the call except that FP8 path, so the budget stays near the in-flight window.

**5. A drop-in runai loader does bypass `DefaultModelLoader` semantics; preserve them by hooking one level lower.**
The installed `RunaiModelStreamerLoader.load_weights` at `results/nvme-loader/inventory/model_executor/model_loader/runai_streamer_loader.py:133` calls `model.load_weights` on its own iterator. What it skips relative to the default loader: index-based duplicate filtering at `default_loader.py:231`, since `list_safetensors` lists every safetensors file in the folder; `allow_patterns_overrides` and the `.pt` fallback; `secondary_weights` sources and their prefixes at `default_loader.py:335`; the EP expert filter at line 425; the torchao strategy; and `track_weights_loading` at line 444, which is the strict missing-weight check for unquantized models, so your unquantized fixtures would not catch a broken checkpoint under the stock runai loader. It does honor `model_config.model_weights`. To keep all of it, subclass `DefaultModelLoader` and override only `_get_weights_iterator` at line 244: call `self._prepare_weights` for file selection, substitute the streaming iterator only when `use_safetensors` is true, wrap it with `should_skip_weight(name, self.local_expert_ids)` from `ep_weight_filter.py:64`, set `counter_before_loading_weights`, and leave `get_all_weights` and `load_weights` untouched. The existing `nvme` loader already subclasses `DefaultModelLoader`, so this is the same class.

**6. Generic slice planning without model hardcodes is available by tracing the native loaders on a meta model.**
The information that decides a slice is not in the checkpoint; it is in what each `weight_loader` does with the tensor it receives. Construct the model once on the meta device, feed `load_weights` meta tensors with the checkpoint shapes from your header scan, and run it under a `TorchDispatchMode` that tracks the affine view chain from the yielded tensor to the source of the final `copy_`: `t`, `transpose`, `slice`, `narrow`, `clone` and `contiguous`. If the chain resolves, the plan for that name is the source rows and columns in checkpoint coordinates; if any other op intervenes, or the loader raises on meta, the plan is "read whole". The installed `CopyCounter` at `results/nvme-loader/inventory/model_executor/model_loader/reload/meta.py:157` is the seed for this. This reproduces the exact native narrowing for every layer type, including the transpose-then-flip MoE case, with no per-model rules, and it also reports the consumer cost of finding 2 before any bytes are read.

## Recommended next implementation

Build mode A of the loader as the first-use and fallback path, keep artifacts for routine switches, and defer cross-node scatter.

1. **Plan** on a meta model as in finding 6, cached next to the source identity so it is computed once per checkpoint and config hash. Output per checkpoint tensor: file, offset, dtype, shape, and either a contiguous extent list or "whole".
2. **Read** with the existing transport primitives: positional `preadv`, 4 KiB-aligned coalesced extents, 32 outstanding, pinned bounce slots. Aligned extents that cover more than about 60 percent of a tensor degrade to a whole-tensor read.
3. **Stage on device.** For each tensor allocate an uninitialized device tensor of the full checkpoint shape, land only the planned extents into it through the bounce slots, and yield it. The native loader then narrows to exactly the landed region, and the transposes and copies run on the GPU. Bound in-flight bytes with the refcount semaphore from finding 4; the 1.9 GB embedding counts as one unit.
4. **Hook** at `DefaultModelLoader._get_weights_iterator` per finding 5 so file selection, secondary sources, EP filtering and weight tracking are unchanged.
5. **Publish** the artifact after `load_weights` and before postprocessing as today, but parallelize the SHA-256 across threads and write with direct I/O so the one-time export is tens of seconds rather than minutes.
6. **Verify** with the existing tiny Llama and GPT2 parity fixtures plus one GLM restart, comparing loaded parameter bytes against a native load rather than only generated tokens.

Expected first-use cost on this cluster: 16 to 50 s of I/O depending on which bandwidth figure holds, plus about 20 to 30 s of consumer Python and loader work, plus the export. Routine switches stay on the 8 s artifact restore. Cross-node owner reads with sliced scatter is the only design that removes the remaining amplification, and it reuses this plan; treat it as a later phase once the world-group point-to-point path is validated inside `load_weights`.

The draft is sound in its facts and its ranking. Seven corrections are material enough to change the implementation, and the ordering needs one merge. Then it can be closed.

**Corrections**

1. **Do not credit the native mmap path with saving bytes on this checkpoint.** The draft says memory mapping "can avoid touching some non-local slices." That only holds for dense dim-0 slices, a few percent of bytes. For 95 percent of bytes the installed MoE callback runs `.t().contiguous()` on the whole tensor at `results/nvme-loader/inventory/model_executor/layers/fused_moe/routed_experts.py:663` before narrowing, so every page is touched. Dim-1 dense slices touch every row's pages too. The native baseline reads all 405 GB; the streamer does not add amplification relative to it.

2. **Rank 1 as written will be consumer-bound, not disk-bound.** The Run:ai iterator yields CPU tensors and clones them into pageable memory at `weight_utils.py:1035`. The MoE transposes then run on the CPU over roughly 380 GB of packed int32, and every H2D copy comes from pageable memory. Two fixes belong in the rank 1 design: clone into pinned memory rather than the default, and try the installed wrapper with `device="cuda:0"` and `is_distributed=False`. In `results/nvme-loader/inventory/runai_safetensors_streamer.py:23` those two parameters are independent; only vLLM's iterator ties them together. If the C++ side accepts a non-distributed device target, transposes and narrows move to the GPU and the consumer cost collapses to Python overhead. If it does not, expect placement rather than NVMe to set the rate, exactly as the draft's throughput inequality predicts.

3. **The list of bypassed default-loader semantics is incomplete.** Add the EP expert filter passed as `local_expert_ids` at `default_loader.py:291`, `allow_patterns_overrides`, the `.pt` fallback, and the torchao load strategy. Overriding `_get_weights_iterator` at line 244 preserves all of these plus the secondary sources, prefixes, index filtering and weight tracking the draft already names. The existing `nvme` loader is already the right class for that override.

4. **Selective reads do not require placement adapters for the common cases.** The draft is right that a pre-sliced tensor gets sliced twice by an unmodified callback. The way around it is to allocate the full checkpoint shape and materialize only the planned extents; the native callback then narrows to exactly the landed bytes and never reads the rest. With device staging the wasted transpose over unlanded rows is cheap. This merges ranks 2 and 3 and drops the "requires adapters" limitation. Adapters remain an optional optimization for transposing only the slice.

5. **Meta-device tracing should be framed as conservative, not as insufficient.** It cannot discover value-dependent loader behaviour, but it does not have to. Any name whose path from the yielded tensor to the final `copy_` is not an affine chain of `t`, `transpose`, `slice`, `narrow`, `clone` and `contiguous`, or whose loader raises on meta tensors, degrades to a whole-tensor read, which is native semantics. That is a generic, model-free planner, and the same dry run reports the consumer's per-tensor work before any bytes are read.

6. **The expert-mapping lookup cannot be compiled from the loader.** The linear scan lives inside the installed model's `load_weights` at `results/nvme-loader/inventory/models/deepseek_v32/nvidia/model.py:375`, and the mapping list is rebuilt on every call. Only an overlay of that model file changes it, which is model-specific. For 175,104 expert tensors against about 768 mapping entries, budget roughly 15 to 25 s per rank as a fixed cost of the dynamic path on GLM. The prepared artifact avoids it entirely, which is the strongest argument for keeping artifacts as the routine path.

7. **Fill in the "layout-dependent" row.** From the layout scan, gate and up slices are contiguous and down slices are not, so selective local reads land near two thirds of expert bytes at 1x and one third at 4x, about 200 GB per rank including the dense layers. At the supplied 4 to 5 GB/s that is 40 to 50 s of read time; at the rate the artifact restore observed it is about 16 s. This is derivable from the headers alone and belongs in the table with that derivation.

Minor wording: "the non-transposed TP4 case" in the down-projection example is confusing because the installed callback transposes first; in checkpoint coordinates the rank's slice is 64 of 256 columns per row either way, and the arithmetic is right.

**Recommended implementation order**

1. Streaming mode as an override of `_get_weights_iterator` in the existing loader class, preserving every default-loader semantic in correction 3. First backend is the installed Run:ai streamer with a finite memory limit at or above the 1.9 GB largest tensor, pinned or device clones per correction 2, and zero-element tensors synthesized from validated headers in header order. No source identity or export required; fresh activation and KV namespace until identity exists. Gate on the tiny-model parity fixtures, then GLM plus the DFlash draft under TP4 and DCP2.
2. Second backend is the project's own range reader: header plan, 4 KiB-aligned coalesced extents, 32 outstanding positional reads through pinned bounce slots, full-shape device staging tensors materialized only where planned, refcount-owned lifetime with a byte semaphore, whole-read as the default and meta-traced slices where the chain resolves.
3. Artifact capture stays synchronous after `load_weights` and before postprocessing, optional per launch, with parallel hashing and direct writes. Background capture is not possible for the pre-kernel format, as the draft correctly states.
4. Cross-node owner reads with sliced scatter last, reusing the plan from step 2 and the world-group point-to-point path inside `load_weights`. It is the only design that removes the residual 2x, and it is not needed for a functional first-load path.

With those corrections applied, the draft can be closed as the record for this investigation. Production is untouched, the prepared-artifact path remains the routine switch mechanism, and the dynamic path becomes the first-use and generic fallback path.
