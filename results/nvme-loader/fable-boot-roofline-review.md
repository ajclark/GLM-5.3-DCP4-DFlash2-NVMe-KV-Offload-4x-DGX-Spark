**Summary:** the document's arithmetic is sound where it is stated, but its ranking rests on an unstated assumption that the current stream is near the storage limit. The project's own numbers show it is not: the artifact reader moved 101.39 GB in 8.03 s on the same drives, about 12.6 GB/s, while the Run:ai stream sustains 6.7 to 6.9 GB/s. The stage metrics in `results/nvme-loader/stream-results.json` explain why, and they change the near-term order.

## Confirmed errors and omissions

**1. The stream is not disk-bound; the document never uses its own stage metrics.** Rank 0, final run: 59.10 s total, of which the pump waited on the reader 20.8 s, spent 18.3 s in blocking uploads, and waited on the consumer 13.9 s. A disk at its roofline would show waiting on the reader dominating. Instead the three stages are balanced, and the reader path delivers about half of what the O_DIRECT artifact reader achieved on the same devices. The likely cause is request granularity: the installed wrapper at `results/nvme-loader/inventory/runai_safetensors_streamer.py:41` issues one chunk per tensor, so the target is 177,569 requests averaging 2.3 MB with a third of them 16-byte shape tensors. That is a request-rate limit, not a bandwidth limit. Nothing in the document's rank 1 to 5 addresses it, yet it is the largest confirmed gap to the stated principle.

**2. Rank 5 and rank 3 omit a hard fabric constraint.** The recorded cluster facts state that NCCL all-to-all and point-to-point between non-adjacent ranks fail on this switchless ring; only ring collectives work, which is why DCP uses `ag_rs`. An owner-scatter that sends rank 0's slices to rank 2 directly does not merely "add forwarding cost"; it fails. The only workable forms are ring collectives, which carry broadcast volume, or explicit neighbor-hop forwarding. The document's per-link accounting must be redone for forwarding: for the 76 GB sliced case, each link-direction carries about 50 GB, roughly double the endpoint volume, because opposite-node flows traverse two hops. Rank 3's InstantTensor and fastsafetensors candidates bind the world process group at `results/nvme-loader/inventory/model_executor/model_loader/weight_utils.py:1129` and `:1052`; whether they exchange tensors by collective or by point-to-point decides whether they can run here at all, and that must be read from their installed sources before any four-rank test.

**3. GDR-disabled cost is under-described.** With `GDR 0` every received byte lands in a host bounce buffer and is then copied to the device allocation; on the unified-memory Spark that is still two DRAM passes per byte plus the NIC DMA, on top of the reader and consumer traffic. For the 304 GB broadcast case that is roughly 0.9 TB of extra DRAM traffic per node during load; for the 76 GB sliced case about 0.2 TB. Rank 3's memory statement should carry those numbers, not only the two-times-largest-tensor staging rule.

**4. Rank 1's transpose item is small on the current device path.** In `cuda` mode the compressed-tensors transpose runs on the GPU; 380 GB of packed int32 read and written at device bandwidth is a few seconds, not a first-order cost. Patching the native callback to narrow before transposing is a layer-level change in the installed overlay for at most that saving. The per-tensor dispatch cost it sits next to is the real item: the 768-entry scan at `results/nvme-loader/inventory/models/deepseek_v32/nvidia/model.py:375` over 175,104 expert tensors and the per-tensor `.to()` and loader calls.

**5. Two installed sources the document missed.** `marlin_moe_permute_scales` at `results/nvme-loader/inventory/model_executor/layers/quantization/utils/marlin_utils.py:497` permutes scales in a Python loop per expert, about 40,000 iterations for GLM inside the 14.12 s post-load residual; it vectorizes into one permute per layer. `process_weights_after_loading` at `results/nvme-loader/inventory/model_executor/model_loader/utils.py:123` calls `release_device_memory_under_pressure` once per module; on this integrated GPU it reads system memory each time and may synchronize and empty the allocator cache whenever available memory is below the threshold, which is the normal condition during a 96 GiB load. Both are quantizer or loader level, not model specific.

**6. Minor.** "A separate upload stream already reduced target streaming from roughly 66 to 59 seconds" is one run each; the implementation report says so, the roofline document does not.

## Hypotheses, stated as such

Coalescing chunks to about 64 MiB should move the reader toward the 12.6 GB/s already demonstrated, giving a target-stream floor near 32 to 35 s if the pump and consumer keep up. The pump's 18.3 s of pageable uploads already overlaps reads and would stop being the limit at one upload per chunk. The consumer is then the likely limit: the 13.9 s of backpressure means it was already slower than the pump by that much, and its per-tensor work does not shrink with reader changes. The Marlin permute loop is plausibly 2 to 5 s; the per-module pressure check is unquantified and needs a timestamp around that loop.

## Revised priority

1. **Reader request granularity.** Build coalesced `FileChunks` requests through the installed `DistributedStreamer` API, or feed whole tensors from the project's own O_DIRECT reader, and split into per-tensor device views after one upload per chunk. Generic, no layer semantics, and it targets the only stage shown to be short of a proven hardware number. Owned-budget accounting must count storage, since a retained view holds the whole chunk.
2. **Per-tensor overhead in the consumer.** Index the expert mapping in an overlay of the DeepSeek-family loader, vectorize the Marlin scale permute, and time the per-module pressure check. Without this the pipeline becomes consumer-bound as soon as item 1 lands.
3. **Startup chain and engine init**, 37.7 s and 19.1 s, as the document has them; storage work cannot touch these, and item 6 remains the long-term answer.
4. **Cross-node**, only after items 1 and 2, and only as ring collectives or neighbor forwarding. The full-tensor broadcast variant preserves generic loading; the sliced scatter does not and needs the placement contract, so it should be scoped as a per-layer optimization with full-tensor fallback rather than as the primary path.

The document's rank 4 arithmetic, its 72-second residual, and its 4 to 5 GB/s versus observed separation are correct and should stay. Its health-versus-weights distinction is also correct: the 59 to 61 s weight stream is the only interval the changes above address, and none of them alter the remaining 72 s.

**1. Confirmed: the per-module pressure check fires during this load and serializes postprocessing.**
`release_device_memory_under_pressure` at `results/nvme-loader/inventory/vllm/utils/mem_utils.py:55` releases when the GPU is integrated, reserved-minus-allocated is at least 512 MiB, and available system memory is at or below 20 percent of total, then calls `synchronize` and `empty_cache`. On these nodes the recorded available memory at weight-load time was 13.9 to 14.4 GiB of 128, about 11 percent, so the pressure condition is true for the whole load. The installed driver at `results/nvme-loader/inventory/model_executor/model_loader/utils.py:123` calls it once per quantized module. Each Marlin repack frees the checkpoint-format tensors it replaces, so reserved-minus-allocated crosses 512 MiB within a few modules and the call then synchronizes the device and returns every cached block to the driver, after which the next module's repack must `cudaMalloc` again. Two consequences follow with certainty: postprocessing cannot overlap on the GPU, and the allocator cache is defeated for the duration. What is a hypothesis is the magnitude; it sits inside the 14.12 s residual. Action: an overlay wrapper that logs each release with its duration on the next activation, then a rate limit such as at most one release per two seconds or only when releasable exceeds 4 GiB. Holding freed blocks between consecutive repacks is safe because the next module reuses them immediately, so the OS-thrash rationale is not weakened.

**2. Confirmed: the reader is at half the demonstrated device rate, and the fix is request size.**
The artifact reader moved 101.39 GB in 8.03 s on the same drives; the stream sustains 6.7 to 6.9 GB/s with the pump waiting on the reader 20.8 s of 59.1 s. The wrapper at `results/nvme-loader/inventory/runai_safetensors_streamer.py:41` issues one chunk per tensor, 177,569 requests averaging 2.3 MB. Build coalesced `FileChunks` of about 64 MiB through the same `DistributedStreamer.stream_files` call and split the returned buffer by your header map, or feed whole tensors from the project's own O_DIRECT reader. One upload per chunk also removes most of the 18.3 s of per-tensor blocking copies. Owned-budget accounting must charge the chunk's storage, since a retained view keeps the whole chunk alive. This remains the largest generic lever on the 59 to 61 s weight interval.

**3. Confirmed by the probe, but the saving is memory traffic, not time.**
`results/nvme-loader/boot-roofline-probe.json` shows slice-then-transpose equals transpose-then-slice for all twelve int32 cases and that gate and up per-rank source slices are single contiguous 1.57 MB ranges, while down slices touch all 6,144 rows and read the full 6.29 MB. That validates the 218 GB selective-read estimate and the callback change that transposes only the rank's slice. In `cuda` mode the transpose already runs at device bandwidth, so the time saved is a few seconds at most; the storage reduction from 6.29 MB to 1.57 MB per expert tensor is the real benefit. The probe excludes scales, packing alignment and padding; before patching the installed callback at `routed_experts.py:663`, check the slice boundaries against the pack factor and the scale group size, which for these shapes are both satisfied. Keep it behind the layer-level guard and below items 1 and 2.

**4. Confirmed source, hypothesis on share: consumer per-tensor overhead becomes the limit once the reader is fixed.**
Backpressure of 13.9 s already shows the consumer slower than the pump. The 768-entry mapping scan per expert tensor at `results/nvme-loader/inventory/models/deepseek_v32/nvidia/model.py:375` and the per-expert Python loop in `marlin_moe_permute_scales` at `results/nvme-loader/inventory/model_executor/layers/quantization/utils/marlin_utils.py:497` are the two places to index and vectorize. Both are overlay changes at the model-family and quantizer level, not transport changes.

**5. Confirmed constraint for the cross-node designs.**
Point-to-point NCCL between non-adjacent ranks fails on this switchless ring; only ring collectives or neighbor forwarding work, GDR is off, and per-link traffic for the sliced scatter is about double the endpoint volume because opposite-node flows take two hops.
